Co-Founder & Partnership Framework
A starting framework for Norm Steeg to join HomeRates.ai as Co-Founder — proposed at this stage, not yet finalized — built around two concrete pieces: a whitelabel deployment of HomeRates to Sure First Lending, and Norm's introduction to United Wholesale Mortgage's (UWM) independent broker network and technology.
Status note: Norm Steeg joining as Co-Founder is a proposal under discussion, not a completed decision — nothing here is final until both sides agree. This document exists to make the shape of that proposal concrete enough to react to.
Section 1
What Each Side Brings

HomeRates.ai and Sure First Lending sit at different points in the same loan journey — one is the consumer-facing intelligence layer, the other is the licensed originator who takes a scenario and turns it into a funded loan. Norm's introduction to UWM is what extends this past one brokerage into a national network.

HomeRates.ai — Front of Journey
→
Consumer decision-intelligence platform: live-rate mortgage scenarios, affordability analysis, Property Intelligence, Decision Score
→
Built AI-native — designed to be invoked inside ChatGPT, Claude, and other AI systems consumers already use to start their search, not just a standalone website
→
Operates before application: consumer is exploring, qualifying, comparing options
→
Whitelabel model: a licensed originator's own branding on the consumer experience, "Powered by HomeRates.ai" attribution
Sure First Lending + UWM Network — Back of Journey
→
Sure First Lending: Norm Steeg's own independent mortgage brokerage — licensed origination, underwriting-ready file preparation, closing
→
Operates after a consumer is scenario-ready: application, conditions, clear-to-close, funding
→
UWM: the nation's largest wholesale mortgage lender for 11 consecutive years, funding loans exclusively through independent brokers like Sure First Lending — not a retail lender HomeRates would compete with
→
UWM's own broker technology: BOLT (self-service underwriting, initial approval in minutes) feeding EASE (UWM's loan origination system)
Key insight: A consumer who reaches a Decision Score through HomeRates is an application-ready borrower. Sure First Lending is where that borrower becomes a real, licensed loan file — and UWM is where that file gets priced, underwritten, and funded at wholesale scale. HomeRates creates the borrower; Sure First Lending originates the loan; UWM funds it.
Worth naming directly: UWM already runs a consumer-facing tool of its own — Mortgage Matchup — a broker-search site with an affordability calculator and even a ChatGPT plugin that connects homebuyers to independent brokers nationally. HomeRates isn't a competitor to that — Mortgage Matchup answers "which broker should I talk to," while HomeRates answers "what does this specific property and financing scenario actually mean for me," with a real Decision Score behind it. The two are complementary, and it's a credibility point, not a risk: UWM already understands the value of AI-native, searchable consumer tooling in this exact space.

Section 3
Proposed Arrangement

This has two pieces at very different stages of readiness — one is something Norm can do today as owner of Sure First Lending, the other depends on introductions that haven't happened yet. Keeping them separate matters: nothing here should read as though UWM has already agreed to anything.

Immediate
Component A — Whitelabel to Sure First Lending
HomeRates deploys under Sure First Lending's own branding — Norm's borrowers get the full consumer intelligence experience without HomeRates ever being the visible brand.
What Sure First Lending gets: A modern, AI-native consumer tool — scenarios, affordability, Property Intelligence, Decision Score — under their own name, at no build cost.

What HomeRates gets: A live, real-world whitelabel deployment and proof point — something concrete to show UWM and other brokers, not a hypothetical.
Aspirational
Component B — Introduction to the UWM Broker Network
Norm's relationship and credibility open a conversation with UWM about HomeRates as a value-added tool for its independent broker network — nationally, not just Sure First Lending.
What this depends on: An actual introduction and conversation that hasn't happened yet — this is the opportunity Norm's Co-Founder role is meant to unlock, not a signed deal.

What it could look like eventually: HomeRates offered as a value-added consumer tool to UWM-affiliated brokers, positioned alongside (not against) UWM's own Mortgage Matchup consumer tool.
Why this sequencing is deliberate: Component A doesn't require anyone's permission but Norm's — it can start now and generate a real reference case. Component B is the actual prize, but it's Norm's relationship and credibility that make that conversation possible at all, which is exactly the value a Co-Founder role is meant to compensate.
Section 3B
Lead Intake & Nurture — Turning Component A Into a Working Pilot

At LoanDepot, Norm's Direct Purchase Lead Program (DPLP) leads arrived two ways at once: a warm-transferred call to whichever LO was first available in that territory, and the same lead landing digitally in that LO's pipeline the instant the transfer happened. As broker/owner of Sure First Lending, he now buys those same leads himself — without the corporate call-center-and-CRM stack that used to do this automatically. Most of these leads are rate-shoppers, which is exactly where Sure First Lending's access to UWM wholesale pricing becomes the closing advantage. This section proposes HomeRates as the piece that replaces the "lands in a pipeline instantly" half of that stack — not the call center or the dialer, which stay entirely Norm's own vendor relationship.

Automated — the instant a lead transfers
→
Vendor's transfer email is captured and turned into a lead record the moment the warm transfer happens — no manual data entry
→
Assigned LO is notified immediately, with the borrower's basic info and loan interest already in front of them
→
Where there's enough detail to work with, a live Rate Intelligence snapshot runs automatically — so the callback opens with a real UWM-vs-market pricing story, not a cold conversation
Human — after the LO takes the call
→
Call notes are typed directly into HomeRates against that same lead — never round-tripped through another email
→
Every follow-up, note, and touchpoint builds a running history — the relationship memory carries the lead from first transfer to close
→
The same Property Lookup / Decision Score tools already in this framework pick up once the borrower has a specific home in mind
Open questions for Norm — not decided here: the exact consent chain a purchased lead gave at the vendor and whether it covers contact by Sure First Lending specifically; whether Sure First Lending or HomeRates is the data controller for this lead data and whether that needs a written understanding between the two; confirmation that the vendor's lead format never includes an SSN (HomeRates has a hard, platform-wide rule against ever storing one); and how long lead data is retained and who besides the assigned LO can see it. None of this is resolved — it's flagged here specifically so it gets discussed before anything is built.
Why this is more than convenience: a working lead-intake pilot for Sure First Lending is the concrete proof that Component A isn't just a branded consumer tool — it's operational infrastructure a broker actually depends on. That's the reference case that makes the UWM conversation in Component B a demonstrated capability instead of a pitch deck.
Section 3C
The AI Trust Layer — Why This Is More Than a CRM

Pull-through on purchased mortgage leads is widely quoted at around 2% industry-wide. The fix was never buying more leads — it's that 98 out of every 100 dollars spent on a lead produces nothing, and the real lever is working every lead better. That's also, not coincidentally, the same reason consumers are shifting to AI over salespeople in the first place: AI gets more knowledgeable and more insightful about a specific person over time, and uses that memory to serve them better than a human ever consistently can. HomeRates already proves that pattern works — it just hasn't been pointed at purchased-lead nurture specifically yet.

Already live — consumer side
→
Welcome Home memory is real, running infrastructure — an AI-generated "pick up where you left off" recap combining a person's own recent activity with live rate context
→
Deliberately private to that one consumer — no loan officer ever sees it. That wall is what makes the trust real, not a limitation to design around
→
Discover already lets a consumer send a specific professional structured questions on rate, costs, process, and after-close commitments — and grades the answers
Being adapted — LO side, for DPLP leads
→
The same memory pattern, mirrored on the compliant LO-facing side: the moment Norm opens a lead, an AI-generated summary of exactly where that relationship stands — built from touchpoints, not raw consumer data
→
Discover pointed at a DPLP lead specifically — a proven mechanism for that lead to independently validate Sure First Lending before committing, a direct lever on pull-through rather than a side feature
→
Both get sharper with real use — this is a direction proven by working infrastructure, not a finished feature
Still early: both of these are real, proven mechanisms — not yet purpose-built for DPLP lead nurture specifically. Expect this to sharpen as real leads run through it rather than arriving finished on day one.
